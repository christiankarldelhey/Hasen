# HASEN — How to Play

*A trick-taking game where winning tricks is the tool, not the goal — the real points come from the bets you declare along the way.*

**Players:** 2–4 · **A round:** 5 tricks · **Game ends:** when someone reaches **100** or drops to **−85**

## What is Hasen?

In Hasen you're a rabbit racing through the forest to gather flowers, leaves, berries and acorns — the ingredients for the special recipe orders waiting back at the burrow. Every rabbit has its own tastes, so what's treasure to you is clutter to someone else. You could hop home with your paws full — but before you know what you'll find, you have to **announce out loud what you plan to bring back**, and the forest doesn't forgive braggarts.

Mechanically, Hasen is a **trick-taking game with bets**. Each round you play five cards, one per trick, trying to take the ones that suit you. The twist: **winning tricks isn't the objective, it's the tool**. The real points come from the bets you declare during the round — and then have to make good on.

You can promise to gather a mountain of points, or almost none. To collect only berries. To win exactly one trick and not one more. The later you declare, the better your information — and the more it costs you if you're wrong anyway.

## The deck

35 cards, 4 suits. **Flowers is always the trump suit.** It's not chosen, not drawn, not negotiated: in every trick of every round, Flowers rules.

Every card worth something shows its points inside a laurel crown in the corner. **If a card has no crown, it's worth 0 points.** It's the handiest rule in the game: no memorizing, just look at the corner.

The court figures — **King, Queen, Ober, Unter** — are helpers who gather more food for you. The three **birds** bring chaos to the table — and, played well, help you too.

## Round setup

At the start of every round:

1. **The bid pool is dealt.** Six bid cards go face-up in the middle: 2 Points, 2 Collection, 2 Tricks. Those six — and only those — can be claimed this round.
2. **Cards are dealt.** You receive 5 cards: 4 hidden in your hand and **1 face-up** in front of you, visible to everyone.
3. The rest of the deck becomes the **draw pile**.

Your face-up card is worth remembering:

- **Everyone sees it**, all round long.
- **It can't be swapped** during the swap phase.
- **It joins your hand** once the swap phase ends, and from then on you play it like any other card.

> *Drawing the Ace of Flowers face-up is a blessing and a curse at once: it gives you the strongest card in the game and tells the whole table you have it.*

## The swap

Before the first trick, **in turn order starting from the lead**, each player picks one of two options:

**A) Swap one card.** Discard one **hidden** card from your hand — it lands face-up in the discard pile for all to see — and draw a fresh random card from the deck.

**B) Pass and bank +3.** Do nothing and take 3 points straight into your game score, right away.

You only get one swap, and the phase only goes around once. When everyone's chosen, the face-up cards join their owners' hands and the first trick begins.

> **Careful with the +3:** it's a score bonus, not card points — so it won't push you into or out of a Points bet. Three points for doing nothing sounds small, but over a game it adds up.

## Playing a trick

A round is **5 tricks**. In each trick, every player plays exactly one card, clockwise, starting from the lead — the *mano*.

**Who leads:**

- In the **first round**, the first player leads; every round after, the lead rotates one seat clockwise.
- Within a round, **the winner of each trick leads the next one** — unless someone played the Woodpecker, who gets to choose.

**The led suit:** the first card played in a trick sets the *led suit*, which becomes the strong suit of that trick (after trumps). One twist: **Flowers can't set the led suit.** If a flower leads, the led suit is decided by the first *non-flower* card played in that trick — and if nobody plays one, the trick has no led suit and only flowers compete.

**You never have to follow suit.** You can play any card you want, always, even holding cards of the led suit. This rule is the heart of the game: in Hasen you never know what the others are holding, and *"I can't, I don't have any"* is not an excuse that exists.

**Resolution:** once everyone's played, cards are compared by the hierarchy below. The winner takes **all the cards in the trick** into their score pile and leads the next trick.

## Card hierarchy

Cards stack into **four levels**. A whole level beats everything below it.

### Level 1 — Major trumps

[[card:flowers-A|Ace of Flowers]] (12) > [[card:flowers-K|King of Flowers]] (11) > [[card:flowers-Q|Queen of Flowers / Karnoffel]] (0)

They beat everything, always, no matter what was led. The only exception in the entire game is the Owl when it leads — and not even then against the Karnoffel.

### Level 2 — Major colors

[[card:berries-O]] [[card:leaves-O]] [[card:acorns-O]] **Ober** > [[card:berries-U]] [[card:leaves-U]] [[card:acorns-U]] **Unter** — **but only if their suit is the led suit.**

If Acorns were led, the Ober of Acorns is a powerhouse. If Leaves were led, that same Ober of Acorns is worth nothing. Same card, two different lives, depending on what was played first.

### Level 3 — Minor trumps

[[card:flowers-5|5]] > [[card:flowers-4|4]] > [[card:flowers-3|3]] > [[card:flowers-2|2]] > [[card:flowers-1|1]] of Flowers

They're trumps: they beat any card of any other suit… **except** the Major colors of the led suit. The humble 1 of Flowers beats the 10 of Berries without breaking a sweat, but loses to the Unter of Berries if Berries were led.

### Level 4 — Minor colors

**10 > 9 > 8 > 7 > 6 > 5**, and only if their suit is the led suit.

| Suit | Cards |
| --- | --- |
| 🫐 Berries | [[card:berries-10]] [[card:berries-9]] [[card:berries-8]] [[card:berries-7]] [[card:berries-6]] [[card:berries-5]] |
| 🍃 Leaves | [[card:leaves-10]] [[card:leaves-9]] [[card:leaves-8]] [[card:leaves-7]] [[card:leaves-6]] [[card:leaves-5]] |
| 🌰 Acorns | [[card:acorns-10]] [[card:acorns-9]] [[card:acorns-8]] [[card:acorns-7]] [[card:acorns-6]] [[card:acorns-5]] |

If their suit **isn't** led, the card doesn't compete at all: it loses to any trump and to any card of the led suit. Decorative garbage — useful for discarding without gifting points.

### Resolving a trick, step by step

1. Any **major trump** (A, K, Q of Flowers)? → The highest one wins. **Done.**
2. Any **Ober or Unter of the led suit**? → The Ober wins; if none, the Unter. **Done.**
3. Any **minor trump** (1–5 of Flowers)? → The highest number wins. **Done.**
4. Otherwise, the **highest card of the led suit** wins.

*(The Owl's exception resolves before all of this — see the next section.)*

> **Example — the 2 of Flowers that ruins the party.** Berries are led. Ana plays the 10 of Berries (led suit, high card, juicy 10 points). Beto plays the Ober of Leaves — nothing: Leaves isn't the led suit, it doesn't compete. Cami plays the **2 of Flowers** and climbs over everyone — a trump is a trump, even a sad little 2. Dani's 8 of Berries is a minor color and loses to the trump.
>
> **Cami wins** and takes 10 + 4 + 2 + 0 = **16 points** in four cards. Beto's Ober just gifted 4 points to a rival without winning anything — classic rookie mistake.

> **Same trick, one Ober played right.** Berries led again. Ana plays the 10 of Berries. Beto plays the **Ober of Berries** — major color of the led suit, straight to the front. Cami's 2 of Flowers, a minor trump, **loses** to the led-suit Ober. Dani's Unter of Berries is also a major color, but the Ober beats it.
>
> **Beto wins.** Same 2 of Flowers, opposite result. The only thing that changed is which suit was led.

## The three birds

The three **S** cards are worth 0 points, and two of them can never win a trick. They're still the best cards in the game. Their rules are printed on the cards, so there's nothing to memorize.

### 🦉 The Owl — *Berries S*

> *If played first, it wins the trick.*

- **If it leads the trick:** it beats absolutely everything — **except the Karnoffel** (Queen of Flowers).
- **If it doesn't lead:** it's just a minor color of Berries — the lowest one, below the 5 — and only competes if Berries were led.
- **It can't open the first trick of the round.** The owl needs a moment to wake up.

**What it's for:** closing out a round with a guaranteed trick, or leading a trick knowing it's yours… unless somebody is sitting on the Queen.

> **Example.** It's trick 4 and Cami leads. She opens with **the Owl**. Ana throws the Ace of Flowers, Beto the 10 of Berries, Dani the Ober of Berries. **Cami wins** with a 0-point card and takes 12 + 10 + 4 = **26 points**. The Ace of Flowers just lost to a bird.
>
> **Now the counter.** Same trick, but Beto was holding the **Queen of Flowers**. He drops it. **Beto wins**, takes the same 26 points, and Cami burned her best card for nothing. That's why the Karnoffel is worth zero points and still nobody discards it.

### 🪶 The Sparrow — *Leaves S*

> *Steal a card from the trick.*

- **It can never win the trick.**
- When the trick ends, **before the winner collects**, you pick **one card from that trick** and take it into your personal stash. It counts as yours for everything: card points and Collection bets.
- The winner takes what's left — including the Sparrow. (Yes, you can steal the Sparrow itself. It's a Leaves card; that's occasionally the whole point.)

**What it's for:** pocketing the Ace of Flowers without having anything to win it with — or pulling out exactly the card a rival needed.

> **Example.** Leaves are led. Ana plays the Ace of Flowers and is winning. Beto plays the **Sparrow**. Cami and Dani toss garbage. Ana wins the trick… but Beto lifts the **Ace of Flowers** out of it into his stash. Ana wins a trick worth 0 points; Beto loses the trick and banks 12.

### 🪵 The Woodpecker — *Acorns S*

> *At the end of the trick, choose who leads the next one.*

- **It can never win the trick.**
- When the trick ends, **you** decide who leads the next trick — no matter who won this one. You can pick yourself.
- If you play it on the **last trick**, it does nothing (there is no next trick).

**What it's for:** dismantling someone's plan. Whoever leads decides the led suit, and whoever decides the led suit decides which cards compete and which don't. It's the most political card in the deck.

> **Example.** Beto is winning trick 3 and needs to lead trick 4 with Acorns so his Ober of Acorns counts. Dani plays the **Woodpecker** and names **Ana** as the next lead. Ana leads Leaves. Beto's Ober of Acorns turns into a piece of cardboard.

## Betting

Winning tricks is fine. Settling bets is where the money is.

### When you can bet

There are **three betting windows**, one in each of the first three tricks. A window is open **while that trick is in progress** — and in this app, you place your bet **on your turn, before playing your card**.

Once trick 3 ends, the kiosk closes: tricks 4 and 5 are played with no betting at all. And if a window passes you by, it's gone — there's no catching up between tricks.

### How to bet

1. Tap one of the six bid cards on the table.
2. Your rabbit lands on it. You're committed.

The restrictions:

- **One bet per window** — three rabbits, three bets, no more.
- **Bets must be from different categories.** No doubling up on Points.
- **You have to make good on all of them**, at once. No choosing which one to save at the end.
- **Every bet is public.** Nothing is hidden here.
- **Max 2 players per bid card.** The third one who wants it has to pick another — no hard feelings, no extra penalty for sharing.

### What failing costs

The penalty depends on **the window where you declared**, not on when it resolves:

| Category | Window 1 | Window 2 | Window 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 🟡 Collection | −10 | −15 | −20 |
| 🔵 Points and 🔴 Tricks | −20 | −25 | −30 |

For Collection bets those numbers are **per forbidden card** you end up holding — not a flat fine.

> **Why wait.** In window 1 you bet almost blind: you've seen your hand and everyone's face-up card. By window 3 you've watched three full tricks, you know which trumps are gone, and you can bet nearly on sure thing. The game charges you for that information. The question isn't *"do I dare?"* — it's *"how much information do I need for this bet to be safe, and can I afford the price?"*

## The bids

Each round only **2 cards per category** are on the table, drawn at random — so part of the game is adapting your hand to what came out. Think of them as **recipe orders**: promises about what you'll bring back from the forest, priced by how hard they are to keep.

### 🔵 Points — *"I know exactly how much I'll score"*

You predict how many **card points** you'll have at round end — everything you won in tricks plus whatever the Sparrow brought home.

| You need | It pays |
| --- | --- |
| 11–30 points | +40 |
| 22–40 points | +45 |
| 5–20 points | +50 |
| 3–15 points | +55 |
| 35 points or more | +60 |
| 2–10 points | +65 |
| 45 points or more | +70 |

The ranges **overlap on purpose** — with 12 points you'd satisfy three different bets. Notice the payout pattern: the tighter and more uncomfortable the promise, the more it pays. Scoring 45+ is a flex; scoring between 2 and 10 while holding winning cards is a high-wire act, and it's priced like one.

> **Example.** You bet **2–10** in window 1. You're coasting at 6 points into the last trick, but the only card left in your hand is the **King of Flowers**. You have to play it. It crushes everything, adds 11, and you finish with 17. Failed: **−20**. You won a trick you didn't want with a card you couldn't not play.
>
> **Moral:** in the low bets, the problem isn't bad cards. It's good ones.

### 🟡 Collection — *"I want these, I don't want those"*

Collect cards of one suit, avoid another. **Every** card that lands in your pile counts — trick wins and Sparrow steals alike.

The seven combinations that exist: Acorns/no-Leaves · Acorns/no-Berries · Berries/no-Acorns · Berries/no-Leaves · Leaves/no-Berries · Leaves/no-Acorns · **Flowers/no-everything-else**.

**How it's counted:**

`NET = (wanted-suit cards × 10) − (forbidden-suit cards × your window's penalty)`

You make the bet if the **net is 10 or more**, and you score the net. Fail it and it always costs you: **at least −10**, and if the net is even lower, you pay the whole thing.

**This bet doesn't look at points — it looks at card counts.** A worthless 8 of Berries counts exactly the same as the 10. It's the one category where garbage is gold.

> **Example A — comfortable.** You bet "Berries / no Leaves" in **window 1**. You finish with 4 Berries and 1 Leaf: `4 × 10 = 40` minus `1 × 10 = 10` → **net 30**. Made it with room: you score **30**.
>
> **Example B — same pile, three tricks later.** Same bet in **window 3**. That single Leaf now costs you 20: `40 − 20` → **net 20**. Still made, but waiting cost you 10 points.
>
> **Example C — on the edge.** Window 2, you end with 3 Berries and 2 Leaves: `30 − 30` → **net 0**. Zero is less than ten: **failed**, and it costs you **−10**. One more card of either suit would have flipped the result.

### 🔴 Tricks — *"I know exactly which tricks I'll win"*

| You have to | It pays |
| --- | --- |
| Win exactly 3 tricks | +40 |
| Win tricks 4 and 5 (earlier ones don't matter) | +45 |
| Win exactly 1 trick | +50 |
| Win 4 or 5 tricks | +55 |
| Lose tricks 1–3 and win 4 and 5 | +60 |
| Win **only** trick 5 — nothing else | +65 |
| Win all 5 tricks | +70 |

**"Exactly 1 trick" is this category's beautiful trap.** It looks like the easiest bet on the board and it's where most people fall. Winning a single trick sounds trivial until you remember you also have to **lose** the other four — and nobody is obliged to follow suit, including the rival who'd love to hand you a trick you didn't want.

> **Example.** You bet on winning exactly one trick. You take trick 2 with the Karnoffel: 1 for 1, perfect. You reach trick 5 holding the 3 of Flowers — all you have left. You play it. Nobody else had a trump. You win the trick you didn't want and lose the bet **for having too many good cards**.
>
> **How to play it well:** win your trick **early**, then hold nothing but garbage. Off-suit color cards you'll never lead are your best friends.

## Scoring a round

After the fifth trick resolves, everyone counts their round score:

1. **Did you bet this round?**
   - **No bets** → you take **the card points** in your pile (including your Sparrow stash).
   - **You bet** → your card points **don't score**: they were the measuring stick for your bets. Your score comes from the bets.
2. **Add what you collected** for each bet you made.
3. **Subtract what you owe** for each bet you failed (by the window where you declared it).
4. The **+3** for skipping the swap already went into your total when you took it.

Then the deck is shuffled, rabbits are freed, the lead rotates one seat clockwise, and the next round begins.

> **This is the biggest decision in the game.** Not betting is a legitimate play: it banks the points of whatever you won, risk-free, with no high ceiling. Betting trades those points for a promise. A round where you won three tricks and 30 points is worth 30 if you kept quiet — and worth 0 if you bet Collection and fell short.

## Game end

The game ends as soon as a player **reaches 100 points** — or **drops to −85**. The forest giveth and the forest taketh away.

**The winner is whoever has the highest total score** — which isn't always the one who crossed the line first, since anyone can land a +70 in the last round.

**Ties:** if players finish level, the tied player **closest to the lead** in turn order wins.

## Fine print

The edge cases that usually start arguments at the table:

- **The Owl didn't lead the trick — what is it?** A minor color of Berries, and the lowest one (below the 5). It only competes if Berries were led.
- **Can the Sparrow steal itself?** Yes. It's worth 0 points, but it's a Leaves card — legal, small, occasionally brilliant.
- **Does the Sparrow steal before or after the winner collects?** Before. The trick resolves, the thief picks a card, and only then does the winner take what's left.
- **Can the Woodpecker pick itself?** Yes, no restrictions.
- **Woodpecker on trick 5?** It fizzles — there's no next trick. It's a 0-point card you're discarding.
- **I led a Flower — what's the led suit?** The first non-flower card played in that trick decides it. If the whole trick ends up being flowers, there's no led suit and the highest flower simply wins.
- **Can I bet during trick 4 or 5?** No — windows close when trick 3 ends.
- **Can I bet after playing my card?** No — in the app, your bet goes down on your turn, before your card hits the table.
- **Three of us want the same bid card.** Only two rabbits fit. The third picks another bet or saves the rabbit for a later window.
- **I forgot to bet in a window.** It's gone. Each window closes with its trick and never reopens.
